# Interview Day and Follow-Up

Preparation is done by the morning of the loop; what is left is execution and a short tail of logistics that candidates fumble more often than the material. This page covers the last 24 hours and the 24 after: what to check the night before, what to do between rounds, and the follow-up messages that keep the process moving.

## The night before

- **Confirm the logistics in one pass:** building and room or video link, interviewer names, agenda with times, and your recruiter's phone number for when something goes wrong.
- **Re-read your own story inventory**, not new material: the self-introduction and the STAR stories from [Behavioral Interviews](behavioral.md). New prep the night before costs sleep and adds nothing.
- **Warm up, do not cram:** one easy problem from early in the [study plan](study_plan.md) to get the syntax flowing, ideally one you have already solved.
- **Skim the [coding cheatsheet](coding_cheatsheet.md)** once: the 30-second checklist and the corner-case shotgun are the parts worth reloading.
- **Prepare the room:** charged laptop, headphones, water, and a quiet space booked for longer than the loop; for onsites, plan the route and arrive early rather than on time.
- **Stop early.** Sleep beats one more problem; the loop scores a rested brain reading a problem carefully, not tonight's last rep.

## Between rounds

The loop is scored as a set of rounds, and the half hours between them are part of the test:

- **Let a bad round go before the next one starts.** Interviewers score what they see, not what preceded it; a reset is a skill, and the next round is a fresh scorecard.
- **Drink water and eat the snack** you brought; four hours of concentration is a physical event.
- **Note nothing about content on paper or screen** that you would not say aloud: in shared documents and open offices, assume the next interviewer can see your notes.
- **Do not re-litigate rounds with your shadowers or your phone;** the time between rounds is for resetting, and nothing new is learnable in it.
- **If something went wrong mechanically** (audio died, broken link, an interviewer who had to leave), tell the recruiter in the gap and ask for it to be noted; a lost round to a dropped call should be retaken, not scored.

## After the loop

- **Send the thank-you notes the same day**, short and specific; they arrive while the interviewers are writing their feedback (the template is below).
- **Tell every other company in your pipeline** that you have interviewed and expect a decision soon; a closing window accelerates them, and the mechanics are the same as the ones in [Salary Negotiation](salary_negotiation.md).
- **Write down what was asked** while it is fresh, both for the debrief habit from [Mock Interviews](mock_interviews.md) and because a rejected loop becomes next quarter's preparation list.
- **Follow up once, then stop.** If the promised date for a decision passes, one polite ping to the recruiter is right; more is noise.
- **On a rejection, ask for feedback** where the process offers it, thank them, and move on. Loops are noisy; the prepared candidate re-enters the market with everything they built still theirs.

## The follow-up email template

Send one to each interviewer, or one to the recruiter to forward where interviewers are anonymous. Keep it under a hundred words; the goal is to be easy to root for, not to add an argument you forgot to make in the room:

```text
Subject: Thank you - [role] interview, [date]

Hi [interviewer name],

Thanks for the conversation today. I especially enjoyed the
discussion of [one specific thing from your conversation], and it
is the kind of problem I would be glad to work on here.

One thought that came up: [optionally, one short point you did
not get to finish, in a sentence or two].

Looking forward to hearing about next steps.

Best regards,
[your name]
```

Three rules for the optional middle paragraph: use it to complete an answer you were cut off from, to correct something materially wrong, or to omit it entirely; never to re-argue a round or to add the fifteenth thing you thought of on the drive home.
